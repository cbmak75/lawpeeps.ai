HM Courts & Tribunals Service has launched a formal study comparing its in-house AI transcription system against commercial providers, in what appears to be the first major test of whether UK departments will build rather than buy legal AI solutions.

The Justice Transcribe pilot will benchmark the government-developed system against existing commercial vendors on accuracy and cost across Crown Court proceedings. If successful, the Ministry of Justice plans to offer free sentencing transcripts to victims from spring 2027, replacing a system where transcripts currently cost hundreds or thousands of pounds.

This represents a direct challenge to commercial transcription contracts across the court system. The study explicitly measures Justice Transcribe against commercial accuracy standards, with findings feeding into what HMCTS describes as "nationwide plans to upgrade, modernise and open up the court system."

The move follows a pattern emerging across Whitehall. The Department for Transport has developed its own Consultation Analysis Tool rather than procuring commercial AI services. The Cabinet Office has signalled interest in bespoke government AI for sensitive workflows where data sovereignty and cost control matter more than speed to market.

For legal services firms holding MoJ technology contracts, the procurement signal is clear. Successful deployment of Justice Transcribe would reduce government spend on transcription services, potentially affecting renewal strategies for existing providers.

The pilot scope currently covers sentencing remarks, but internal documents suggest the system could expand to full trial transcripts if accuracy thresholds are met. HMCTS has not disclosed these thresholds, but the comparison with commercial providers suggests they align with existing industry standards rather than setting new benchmarks.

The access to justice dimension matters beyond procurement. Current transcript costs create genuine barriers for victims and families seeking court records. Free provision would remove this barrier, but only if the AI system can match human transcription accuracy for legal proceedings where precision is critical.

The timing is notable. Commercial legal AI providers have raised significant funding this year on the promise of government contracts, but this pilot suggests departments are questioning whether to remain customers or become competitors.

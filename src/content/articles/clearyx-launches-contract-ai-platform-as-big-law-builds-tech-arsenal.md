ClearyX, the alternative legal services provider created by New York firm Cleary Gottlieb, has launched CX+, a contract insights and due diligence platform that puts the Big Law subsidiary directly into the contract AI market.

The platform promises contract analysis capabilities, though ClearyX has not detailed specific features or pricing. According to Artificial Lawyer, which reported the launch yesterday, the tool is positioned for due diligence work alongside broader contract insights.

ClearyX represents Cleary Gottlieb's attempt to capture efficiency gains and client work that might otherwise flow to standalone legal tech companies or other ALSPs. The firm established the subsidiary to handle lower-complexity work at reduced rates while maintaining the Cleary brand connection.

The contract AI space has become increasingly crowded, with established players like Kira Systems and newer entrants competing for law firm adoption. ClearyX's entry suggests major firms view AI-powered contract review as too strategically important to outsource entirely.

The timing aligns with broader patterns of Big Law technology investment. Firms are building internal capabilities rather than simply licensing third-party tools, particularly for high-volume, repeatable work like contract review and due diligence.

I could not verify specific technical details about CX+ beyond the initial announcement. ClearyX has not published detailed product information on its website, and the company did not respond to requests for additional information about capabilities or client availability.

*The subsidiary model for legal AI is spreading. Whether it delivers better outcomes than pure-play vendors remains an open question. mm!ke*
